Ongoing Research

Advancing Modern Hip & Knee Care Through Research

Dr. Harris is actively involved in ongoing research that helps improve modern hip, knee, and perioperative care. Staying closely engaged with the evidence, and contributing to it, helps keep his patients at the cutting edge of proven, up-to-date care and supports the best possible outcomes. He has authored more than 100 peer-reviewed publications on hip and knee replacement and serves as a peer reviewer for multiple orthopaedic journals. His work asks practical questions with a single aim: making surgery safer, more comfortable, and longer-lasting. An up-to-date list of his publications can be found on Google Scholar.

Preventing Infection

Infection is one of the most serious complications of joint replacement. Dr. Harris’s research looks at who is most at risk and at the preoperative steps, from antibiotic strategies to correcting risk factors that can help lower that risk.

Trends in extended oral antibiotic prophylaxis after primary and revision total hip replacement, 2010–2022. The Journal of Arthroplasty
Preoperative hemoglobin strata that predict transfusion also predict major complications and periprosthetic joint infection. J. of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons

Preventing Fractures

Bone weakened by osteoporosis can fracture around an implant, sometimes years later. Dr. Harris studies how bone health shapes implant durability, and how recognizing and treating it can protect patients from fractures around a hip or knee replacement.

The growing burden of periprosthetic fractures after total hip replacement: identifying trends and at-risk groups. Osteoporosis International
Osteoporosis and total knee replacement: higher five-year implant-related complications. The Journal of Arthroplasty

Reducing Blood Transfusions

Blood loss during surgery can mean a transfusion and a slower recovery. Dr. Harris’s research helps identify patients at risk of anemia and examines techniques used to limit blood loss and the need for transfusion.

Risk-stratified use of tranexamic acid in hip and knee replacement over the past decade. Bulletin of the Hospital for Joint Diseases
Preoperative anemia as a growing predictor of transfusion after total knee replacement. The Journal of Arthroplasty

Knowing the Risks Before Surgery

Not every patient carries the same risk, and much of that can be measured beforehand. Dr. Harris uses routine preoperative bloodwork to define where risk actually rises, so that conditions such as diabetes and kidney disease can be optimized before the day of surgery rather than discovered afterward.

Blood-sugar thresholds that predict 90-day major complications after knee replacement. The Journal of Arthroplasty
Kidney function thresholds that predict 90-day major complications after knee replacement. J. of the American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons
