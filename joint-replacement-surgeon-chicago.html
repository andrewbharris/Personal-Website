Joint Replacement Surgeon in Chicago

Andrew B. Harris, MD is a fellowship-trained joint replacement surgeon in the Chicago area, focused on hip and knee replacement.

As a joint replacement surgeon in the Chicago area, Dr. Harris cares for patients with arthritis and other conditions of the hip and knee, from first-time joint replacement to complex and revision surgery. His practice is focused on the hip and knee, which means the operations on this page are the operations he performs routinely.

Choosing a surgeon for a hip or knee replacement is a decision most people make once. The following questions may be important in helping choose a surgeon.

  • Hip replacement in Chicago, including the anterior and STAR approaches
  • Knee replacement in Chicago, total and partial, often robotic-assisted
  • Revision joint replacement for implants that have worn, loosened, or become infected
  • Nonoperative treatment of hip and knee arthritis

Dr. Harris sees patients in Skokie, across the North Shore, and in the city of Chicago.

What to look for in a hip or knee replacement surgeon

Subspecialty focus and training

Hip and knee replacement is a subspecialty within orthopaedic surgery. Some surgeons complete an additional year of fellowship training in adult reconstruction after residency and then limit their practice to these operations.

It is worth asking how much of a surgeon’s practice is hip and knee replacement, because it shapes how often they meet the less common problems: unusual anatomy, previous fractures, and dealing with problems years later if the implant that has to come out and be revised.

Dr. Harris completed his residency at The Johns Hopkins Hospital and his fellowship in adult reconstruction at the Hospital for Special Surgery. His practice is limited to the hip and knee, and he has authored more than 100 publications and presentations on hip and knee replacement.

How the decision gets made

This is the part a patient can actually judge, and it may be the most useful signal available.

A surgeon should be able to explain why an operation is being recommended now rather than in a year, which non-surgical options exist and whether they have had a real trial, and what specifically about the examination and the imaging is driving the recommendation. It is also worth asking what happens if you wait.

A joint replacement is an elective operation. There is no point at which it becomes mandatory. A surgeon who tells you plainly that the timing is yours to choose, and who is willing to say that surgery is not the right step yet, is giving you more than one who does not.

Second opinions

A second opinion is most useful when the recommendation is not straightforward. That includes imaging and symptoms that do not line up, a revision surgery being discussed, or two surgeons proposing different operations.

Second opinions are less useful for timing. Whether to have a straightforward replacement now or in a year is a decision that belongs to the patient rather than something a second surgeon can settle.

Surgeons are used to being asked these questions, and asking about a second opinion generally should not negatively affect the patient-physician relationship.

Frequently asked questions

What does a joint replacement surgeon do?

A joint replacement surgeon evaluates hip and knee problems and, when non-surgical care is no longer enough, replaces the worn joint surfaces with implants. Dr. Harris focuses specifically on hip and knee replacement, including complex and revision surgery.

Should I get a second opinion?

It depends what the question is. Second opinions are most useful when the recommendation is not straightforward, such as when a revision is being discussed or when two surgeons have suggested different operations. They are less useful for deciding when to have a routine replacement, since that timing is yours.

Do I need a referral?

Requirements vary by insurance plan. You are welcome to request an appointment directly, and the office can help you understand what your plan needs.

Where does Dr. Harris see patients in Chicago?

Dr. Harris sees patients in Skokie, on the North Shore, and in the city of Chicago.

Does he treat problems other than arthritis?

Yes. Osteoarthritis is the most common reason for a hip or knee replacement but not the only one. Avascular necrosis, inflammatory arthritis, hip dysplasia, and damage years after an injury or fracture can all lead to the same operation.
