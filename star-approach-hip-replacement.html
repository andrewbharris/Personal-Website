Hip Replacement

STAR Approach Hip Replacement in Chicago

The STAR approach (Superior Transverse Anatomic Reconstruction) is a modern, muscle-sparing posterior-based total hip replacement that Dr. Harris trained in at the Hospital for Special Surgery.

The STAR approach is a refined, tissue-sparing take on the traditional posterior hip replacement, designed to protect the muscles and tendons that keep the hip stable.

STAR stands for Superior Transverse Anatomic Reconstruction. It is a posterior-based approach, meaning the hip is reached from the back, and it is a modern evolution of the traditional posterior approach that has been used successfully for decades. Where older posterior techniques often divided the muscles and tendons around the hip, the STAR approach preserves and reconstructs them, which helps lower the chance of the new joint slipping out of place (dislocation) and supports a confident recovery.

Dr. Harris trained in the STAR approach at the Hospital for Special Surgery, regarded by many as one of the top institutions in the world for training orthopaedic surgeons. It is an excellent approach with proven, high-quality outcomes and low dislocation rates. It is one of the techniques Dr. Harris uses for hip replacement in Chicago, including the direct anterior approach. The right approach for you depends on your anatomy, your imaging, and your health history, not a single-technique philosophy.

When a posterior-based approach makes sense

A posterior-based approach such as STAR may be recommended when your anatomy, prior surgery, or specific hip condition make it the safer or more reliable choice. Dr. Harris will explain clearly why one approach is being recommended over another.
