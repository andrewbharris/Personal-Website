Patient Guide

Hip Replacement Recovery: What to Expect

A clear, realistic look at hip replacement recovery, week by week, from Dr. Harris.

Recovery from a hip replacement is steady and, for most people, faster than they expect, especially with the muscle-sparing anterior approach. Most patients are up and walking with help the same day, home within a day or two (or the same day for outpatient surgery), and back to most daily activities within two to three months.

Swelling, stiffness, bruising, and disturbed sleep are all normal early on and improve over weeks. Recovery is real work: following your physical therapy plan matters as much as the operation itself.

A typical timeline

  • Day of surgery: stand and take steps with assistance
  • First 1–2 weeks: walking with a walker or cane, focus on comfort and safe movement
  • 2–6 weeks: less need for walking aids, gradual return to light activity and driving
  • 6–12 weeks: most daily activities resume; strength and endurance keep building
  • Up to a year: continued improvement as you feel the full benefit
Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

How long does hip replacement recovery take?

Most patients walk with assistance the same day or the next day, are moving comfortably around the house within a few weeks, and return to most normal activities within about two to three months. It can take up to a year to feel the full benefit of the new hip.

When can I walk after hip replacement?

Most patients stand and take steps with assistance the day of surgery. Many use a walker or cane for the first few weeks, then progress to walking without aids as comfort and strength return.

When can I drive after hip replacement?

Many patients return to driving around a few weeks after surgery, once they are off strong pain medication and can move safely. Dr. Harris will give guidance based on your progress and which hip was operated on.

Is hip replacement outpatient or same-day?

For many healthy patients, especially with the anterior approach, hip replacement can be done as an outpatient, same-day procedure with recovery continued at home. Whether that is right for you depends on your health and home setup.
