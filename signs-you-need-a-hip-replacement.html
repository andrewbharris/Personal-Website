Signs You May Need a Hip Replacement

Wondering whether your hip pain means it is time for surgery? Here is how Dr. Harris thinks about the signs.

Hip arthritis rarely announces itself all at once. It usually builds: a stiff hip in the morning, a shorter walk before pain sets in, trouble putting on shoes and socks, an ache that starts to interrupt sleep. Because it comes on slowly, many people are surprised how much life they have quietly given up by the time they ask about surgery.

A hip replacement is considered when arthritis pain limits the things that matter to you and non-surgical treatment is no longer enough, not at a particular age or X-ray finding. The decision is always individualized to your symptoms, examination, and imaging. Learn more about the anterior and STAR approaches Dr. Harris performs.

Common signs of hip arthritis

  • Groin, thigh, or buttock pain when walking or standing
  • Stiffness that makes shoes, socks, or getting out of a car hard
  • Pain that disturbs your sleep
  • A limp, or a hip that feels like it is “catching”
  • Less relief than before from rest, medication, or injections
Common Questions

Frequently asked questions

How do I know if I need a hip replacement?

Hip replacement is usually considered when hip arthritis pain limits daily life despite non-surgical treatment. Common signs include groin or thigh pain with walking, stiffness that limits putting on shoes or socks, pain that disturbs sleep, and a hip that no longer responds to activity changes, physical therapy, anti-inflammatory medication, or injections.

What conditions lead to a hip replacement?

The most common cause is osteoarthritis (wear-and-tear arthritis). Others include avascular necrosis (loss of blood supply to the ball of the hip), hip dysplasia, inflammatory arthritis such as rheumatoid arthritis, and certain hip fractures.

Should I try other treatments first?

Yes. Most hip arthritis is managed first without surgery: activity modification, physical therapy, weight management, anti-inflammatory medication, and injections. Surgery enters the conversation when those measures no longer provide enough relief to do the things you care about.

What happens at a hip replacement evaluation?

Dr. Harris reviews your symptoms, performs a hands-on examination, and reviews your X-rays or MRI. Every recommendation is individualized. If surgery is the right step, he will explain the options, including the anterior and STAR approaches.
