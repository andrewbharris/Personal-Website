Hip Replacement

Anterior Approach Hip Replacement in Chicago

Dr. Harris performs anterior approach hip replacement for patients in Chicago, with offices in Skokie and Chicago.

What it is

The hip is a ball-and-socket joint. When the smooth cartilage lining the joint wears away, most often from osteoarthritis, the common “wear-and-tear” form of arthritis, bone begins to rub on bone, causing pain, stiffness, and a shrinking radius of activity. A hip replacement removes the worn ball and socket and replaces them with implants sized and positioned to recreate your anatomy.

Hip replacement is one of the most reliable operations in all of surgery: for most patients it means dramatic pain relief and a return to walking, cycling, golf, travel, and daily life without thinking about the hip. When it adds value, Dr. Harris plans the operation from your own imaging and uses robotic assistance and computer navigation to position the new joint precisely.

When it’s considered

  • Osteoarthritis (wear-and-tear arthritis), by far the most common reason
  • Avascular necrosis, loss of blood supply to the ball of the hip
  • Hip dysplasia, a shallow socket that wears out early, often in younger patients
  • Inflammatory arthritis, such as rheumatoid arthritis
  • Certain hip fractures

The direct anterior approach

The direct anterior approach is the muscle-sparing, minimally invasive technique Dr. Harris prefers for most total hip replacements.

The direct anterior approach reaches the hip from the front, working between the muscles rather than cutting through them. For many patients that can mean less discomfort early on, a quicker return to walking, and added protection against the new joint slipping out of place (dislocation). Many patients go home the same day, and anterior hip replacement is well suited to an outpatient, same-day pathway when appropriate.

When your anatomy is a good fit, Dr. Harris can perform it through a “bikini” incision, an incision that follows the natural crease of the skin, leaving a lower, more discreet scar.

Why patients ask for it

  • Muscle-sparing, the muscles are moved aside rather than detached
  • Minimally invasive, often through a small “bikini” incision
  • Lower dislocation risk compared with some traditional approaches
  • Often outpatient, many patients go home the same day
  • Robotic-assisted planning and computer navigation for precise implant position

The anterior approach is not the only good option, and it is not right for every hip. When your anatomy or history is a better fit for a posterior-based approach, Dr. Harris also performs the STAR approach (Superior Transverse Anatomic Reconstruction), a modern muscle-sparing technique he trained in at the Hospital for Special Surgery.
